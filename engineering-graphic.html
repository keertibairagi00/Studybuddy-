UNIT 01PROJECTION

Engineering Drawing Fundamentals

Engineering graphics communicates shape, size and manufacturing information using standardized drawing methods.

Drawing essentials

  • Use appropriate line types and uniform lettering.
  • Keep dimensions clear and avoid unnecessary crossings.
  • Use a suitable scale when the true size cannot fit conveniently.
  • Construction lines should remain light and final outlines should be distinct.

Scales

Representative fraction (RF) = drawing length / actual length, using the same units. A scale may be full size, reducing or enlarging.

Practical tip: Start construction lightly, check alignment, then darken only the final required edges.
UNIT 02PROJECTION

Orthographic Projection

Orthographic projection represents a three-dimensional object using separate two-dimensional views such as front, top and side views.

Principal views

The front view shows height and width; the top view shows width and depth; the side view shows height and depth. The arrangement of views depends on the projection system used.

First-angle and third-angle projection

In first-angle projection, the object is conceptually between the observer and the plane. In third-angle projection, the plane is conceptually between the observer and the object. Always follow the projection symbol and convention specified in the question.

Exam tip: Draw projection lines carefully and maintain common reference edges between views.
UNIT 03PROJECTION

Projection of Points, Lines & Solids

Projection problems require systematic construction from given positions and dimensions.

Projection of a point

Locate the point relative to the reference planes, then project perpendicular construction lines to obtain its corresponding views.

Projection of a line

Draw the line in the specified position first, then obtain the other view by projecting endpoints. True length may require auxiliary construction when the line is inclined to the reference planes.

Projection of solids

For prisms, pyramids, cylinders and cones, begin with the base in the appropriate plane and project all important vertices/generators systematically.

UNIT 04SECTIONING

Sections of Solids

Sectioning shows the shape exposed when a solid is cut by an imaginary section plane.

Section plane

The section plane is an imaginary plane used to cut the object. The intersection points are projected into the required view to obtain the sectional shape.

Hatching

Cut surfaces are conventionally represented by thin, evenly spaced hatch lines. Keep hatching consistent and avoid excessive density.

Typical solids

Common exercises involve prisms, pyramids, cylinders and cones cut by planes at specified inclinations.

UNIT 05DEVELOPMENT

Development of Surfaces

Development unfolds the surface of a solid onto a plane without changing the true lengths of the developed edges.

Methods

  • Parallel-line method: used for prisms and many prismatic forms.
  • Radial-line method: used for pyramids and cones.
  • Triangulation: useful when true lengths are required for irregular developments.

Applications

Surface development is used in sheet-metal work, ducts, containers and fabrication layouts where a 3D surface must be cut from flat material.

Exam tip: Identify the correct development method before starting the construction.
