UNIT 01FOUNDATION

Communication Process

Communication is the systematic exchange of information, ideas or feelings between a sender and receiver.

Elements

The basic process includes sender, encoding, message, channel, receiver, decoding and feedback. Noise can interfere with any stage.

Types

TypeMeaning
VerbalUses spoken or written words.
Non-verbalUses gestures, posture, facial expressions and other cues.
FormalFollows an organizational structure or official channel.
InformalOccurs through casual or unofficial interaction.
Exam tip: In a process diagram, show sender → encoding → message/channel → decoding → receiver, with feedback returning to the sender.
UNIT 02SKILLS

Barriers to Communication

Communication barriers reduce clarity, accuracy or understanding between participants.

Major barriers

  • Physical: noise, distance or poor equipment.
  • Semantic: ambiguous words, jargon or different meanings.
  • Psychological: stress, assumptions, prejudice or lack of attention.
  • Organizational: excessive hierarchy, unclear roles or poor information flow.

How to improve

Use simple language, listen actively, confirm important information, choose an appropriate channel and encourage feedback.

UNIT 03WRITING

Business Letters & Professional Writing

Professional writing should be clear, concise, accurate and appropriate for the reader and purpose.

Common structure of a formal letter

Sender details → date → receiver details → subject → salutation → body → complimentary close → signature/name.

Writing principles

  • State the purpose early.
  • Use short, logically ordered paragraphs.
  • Avoid unnecessary slang and repetition.
  • Check grammar, spelling, names and dates before sending.
Useful opening: “I am writing to request…” / “This letter is regarding…” Use a direct opening that matches the purpose.
UNIT 04PROFESSIONAL

Resume & Job Application

A resume presents relevant education, skills, projects and experience in a concise professional format.

Recommended sections

  • Name and professional contact details
  • Career objective or concise profile, when useful
  • Education
  • Technical and soft skills
  • Projects / internships / experience
  • Achievements, certifications or relevant activities

Good practice

Tailor the resume to the role, use action-oriented descriptions for projects and keep formatting consistent. Do not claim skills or experience you do not have.

Exam tip: Learn the format and be ready to write a sample resume for a fresh engineering student.
UNIT 05REPORT

Report Writing & Presentation Skills

A technical report communicates a problem, method, observations and conclusions in a structured form.

Basic report structure

Title → introduction/objective → methodology or procedure → observations/results → discussion → conclusion → references/appendix where required.

Presentation skills

  • Know the objective and audience.
  • Use a simple beginning, middle and conclusion.
  • Keep slides readable and avoid overcrowding them with text.
  • Speak clearly, maintain appropriate eye contact and manage time.
Q: What makes a report effective? Clear purpose, logical structure, accurate information and evidence-based conclusions.
Q: Why is feedback important? It confirms whether the intended message was understood and allows correction of misunderstandings.
