UNIT 01FOUNDATION

DC Circuit Fundamentals

Voltage, current, resistance and circuit laws are the foundation for analyzing electrical networks.

Basic quantities

  • Current (I): rate of flow of electric charge, measured in ampere.
  • Voltage (V): electrical potential difference, measured in volt.
  • Resistance (R): opposition to current, measured in ohm.
  • Power (P): rate of electrical energy conversion, measured in watt.
Ohm's law: V = IR
Power: P = VI = I²R = V²/R

Kirchhoff's laws

KCL: algebraic sum of currents at a node is zero; equivalently, total current entering a node equals total current leaving. KVL: algebraic sum of voltage changes around a closed loop is zero.

Example: If R = 6 Ω and V = 12 V, then I = V/R = 2 A and P = VI = 24 W.
UNIT 02AC

Alternating Current & AC Circuits

An alternating quantity changes magnitude and direction periodically. Sinusoidal AC is the most common waveform in power systems.

Sinusoidal quantities

UNIT 03MACHINES

Transformer

A transformer transfers AC electrical energy between circuits through electromagnetic induction, normally without changing frequency.

Working principle

A changing current in the primary winding creates changing magnetic flux in the core. The changing flux links the secondary winding and induces an emf.

For an ideal transformer: V₂/V₁ = N₂/N₁ and I₂/I₁ = N₁/N₂.

Types

  • Step-up: secondary turns greater than primary turns, so secondary voltage is higher.
  • Step-down: secondary turns fewer than primary turns, so secondary voltage is lower.

Losses

Major losses include copper loss in windings and core losses such as hysteresis and eddy-current loss. Laminated cores reduce eddy-current loss.

UNIT 04MACHINES

Electrical Machines: DC & Induction Motors

Electric motors convert electrical energy into mechanical energy using electromagnetic effects.

DC motor principle

A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force. In a motor, the arrangement of conductors and commutation produces continuous torque.

Three-phase induction motor

A three-phase stator supply creates a rotating magnetic field. This field induces currents in the rotor, producing torque. The rotor runs slightly below synchronous speed in normal operation.

Synchronous speed: Nₛ = 120f/P (rpm), where f is supply frequency and P is number of poles.
Exam tip: For motor questions, include principle, construction, working, applications and a neat labeled diagram.
UNIT 05ELECTRONICS

Basic Electronics

Semiconductor devices control current and form the building blocks of electronic circuits.

PN junction diode

A diode primarily allows current in one direction. Forward bias reduces the depletion barrier and permits substantial conduction; reverse bias widens the depletion region and gives very small current until breakdown conditions.

Rectification

A rectifier converts AC into unidirectional pulsating DC. Half-wave rectification uses one half-cycle; full-wave rectification uses both half-cycles and gives better utilization of the input waveform.

BJT basics

A bipolar junction transistor has emitter, base and collector regions. In active operation it can be used for amplification; as a switch it is operated between cutoff and saturation.
