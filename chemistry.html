UNIT 01CORE

Water Technology

Hardness, water treatment and boiler problems are common engineering-chemistry foundations.

Hardness of water

Hardness is the property that prevents water from forming lather readily with soap. It is mainly caused by dissolved calcium and magnesium salts. Temporary hardness is associated mainly with bicarbonates; permanent hardness is associated with sulphates and chlorides.

  • Temporary hardness: Ca(HCO₃)₂ and Mg(HCO₃)₂.
  • Permanent hardness: CaSO₄, MgSO₄, CaCl₂ and MgCl₂.

Important treatment methods

Boiling: removes temporary hardness by decomposing bicarbonates. Zeolite process: exchanges Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ ions with Na⁺. Ion-exchange process: uses cation and anion exchange resins to produce demineralized water.

Hardness is commonly expressed as mg/L (or ppm) as CaCO₃.
Exam tip: Write the type of hardness, its cause, one example salt and the removal method in a table for a clear long answer.
UNIT 02CORE

Fuels & Combustion

Fuel chemistry connects calorific value, combustion and the practical performance of solid, liquid and gaseous fuels.

Calorific value

The calorific value is the heat released when a unit quantity of fuel burns completely. Higher calorific value (HCV) includes the latent heat of condensation of water vapour; lower calorific value (LCV) excludes it.

Complete vs incomplete combustion

With sufficient oxygen, carbon tends to form CO₂. With insufficient oxygen, incomplete combustion can form CO and soot. Efficient combustion therefore requires appropriate air supply, mixing and temperature.

For a gaseous fuel, calorific value is often reported per unit volume; for solid and liquid fuels, it is commonly reported per unit mass.

Fuel characteristics

  • High calorific value
  • Moderate ignition temperature
  • Low moisture and ash for solid fuels
  • Easy storage, transport and controlled combustion
UNIT 03MATERIALS

Corrosion & Its Control

Corrosion is the gradual deterioration of a material, usually a metal, due to chemical or electrochemical interaction with its environment.

Electrochemical corrosion

In an electrochemical cell, oxidation occurs at the anode and reduction occurs at the cathode. Metal atoms at the anodic region lose electrons and enter solution as ions.

Anode: M → Mⁿ⁺ + ne⁻
Cathode: reduction reaction consumes the electrons.

Methods of prevention

  • Barrier coatings: paint, enamel, polymer or metallic coatings isolate the metal.
  • Galvanizing: zinc coating protects iron/steel.
  • Cathodic protection: the protected metal is made the cathode using a sacrificial anode or impressed current.
  • Alloying: suitable alloying can improve corrosion resistance.
Remember: corrosion involves oxidation of the metal; prevention works by interrupting the electrochemical process or isolating/protecting the metal.
UNIT 04MATERIALS

Polymers & Engineering Materials

Polymers are high-molecular-mass materials formed from repeating structural units called monomers.

Types of polymerization

Addition polymerization joins unsaturated monomers without eliminating a small molecule. Condensation polymerization generally involves functional groups and may eliminate small molecules such as water or HCl.

Thermoplastics vs thermosetting polymers

ThermoplasticsThermosetting
Soften on heating and can be reshaped.Set permanently after curing and do not readily remelt.
Generally linear or branched chains.Highly cross-linked network.
Examples: PVC, polyethylene.Examples: Bakelite, melamine-formaldehyde.

Applications

PVC is used for pipes and cable insulation; polyethylene is used in packaging and containers; epoxy resins are widely used in adhesives, coatings and composites.

UNIT 05ENVIRONMENT

Green Chemistry & Environmental Chemistry

Green chemistry aims to reduce or eliminate hazardous substances and waste at the source rather than treating pollution after it is formed.

Key ideas

  • Prevent waste instead of treating it after formation.
  • Prefer safer chemicals and safer reaction conditions.
  • Use renewable feedstocks where practical.
  • Improve atom economy and energy efficiency.
  • Design products that degrade into less harmful substances after use.

Water pollution indicators

BOD is the oxygen required by microorganisms to biologically oxidize biodegradable organic matter. COD measures oxygen equivalent required for chemical oxidation of oxidizable substances and is generally faster to determine than BOD.

Long-answer structure: definition → principle → examples → engineering application → environmental benefit.
